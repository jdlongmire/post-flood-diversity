# Rate Models

Quantified bounds on how fast diversification mechanisms can operate, for
use against the stipulated time window (`1-boundary/chronology.md`).

## Standing principle

Observed rapid diversification may establish biological possibility or
empirical rate bounds. It may not be treated as a demonstration that the
proposed post-Flood history occurred (charter Rule 1). Rate models live on
the possibility side of that line; applying them to an Ark founder
population is a model claim (`4-models/`), not an evidence claim.

## Current rate references

- Dogs: substantial skull and size diversity over the domestication period,
  extremes concentrated in recent centuries of breeding; still one
  interbreeding species
  (`3-evidence/observed-diversification/dogs.md`).
- Livestock (cattle, sheep, cats): breed-level diversity over the historical
  and archaeological record of domestication, without a new body plan
  (`3-evidence/observed-diversification/livestock.md`).
- Madeira mice: chromosomal races with partial hybrid infertility in
  centuries (`3-evidence/observed-diversification/madeira-mice.md`).
- Darwin's finches: beak shifts within decades; hybrid lineage isolated in
  a few generations
  (`3-evidence/observed-diversification/darwin-finches.md`).

## Not available as rate inputs

- A timed watch of one pair producing a whole plan's species list.
- A transition from one body plan to another.
- A dated duration taken from fossils or molecular clocks (out of scope
  unless added as an auxiliary).
