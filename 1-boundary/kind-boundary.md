# Kind Boundary

Kind width is a **protective-belt choice**, not a hard-core commitment. The
core requires only that the number of preserved units equal the number of
plans treated as primitive.

## The competing hypotheses

- **Body-plan cut.** Few origins, more unpacking required per origin. About
  eight to ten land-vertebrate plans (see `body-plan-candidates.md`). Bats,
  whales, horses, and people sit inside the mammal plan; ostriches and
  hummingbirds inside the bird plan.
- **Family-level cut.** Hundreds to a few thousand origins, less unpacking
  per origin. The usual flood-model alternative.

## Standing rule

Body-plan and family-level hypotheses **compete**; neither is assumed.
PDP-02 (Kind Boundary Project) adjudicates using morphology, reproductive
compatibility, comparative genomics, developmental architecture, and
phylogenetic structure.

Finer clusters (carnivoran jaw, hoof, rodent gnawing set, bat wing) are
treated as modifications of one plan, not extra origins, unless the belt
redraws the kind boundary on evidence.

## Why the distinction matters

Kind width determines how much diversification each founder stock must
produce and how many units the hull must carry. It interacts with the
hull boundary (`ark-boundary.md`), the allele cap (`preserved-stock.md`),
and the severity of the taxon gradient (`0-programme/roadmap.md`): if a
body-plan-level mammalian kind is proposed, deriving bats from that founder
population is a much more severe test than deriving dog breeds from a
canid-like stock.
