# Heuristics

Heuristics guide how the programme meets difficulties: what to try first, what
is forbidden, and how to classify a change.

## Negative heuristic

- Do not explain the origin set by a long, unbottlenecked radiation.
- Do not treat the breeding stock as genetically impoverished.
- From the hull: do not embark the species lists. A species-level stock is
  ruled out by volume, independent of genetics (`1-boundary/ark-boundary.md`).

## Positive heuristic

Sort living land animals by body plan, assign each plan a small founding
stock, and check later variety against diversification already seen inside a
plan.

## Methodological rules (charter-level)

**Rule 1 (observation discipline).** PDP may use observed rapid diversification
to establish biological possibility or empirical rate bounds. It may not treat
such observations as demonstrations that the proposed post-Flood history
occurred. Historical reconstruction and mechanism are separate claims and must
be registered separately.

**Rule 2 (revision discipline).** An auxiliary that resolves one difficulty by
changing the stipulated founder population, chronology, or hard-core historical
event must be classified as a programme revision rather than an ordinary
protective-belt adjustment. Example: giving a founder pair more than four
alleles at an autosomal locus changes the origin stock; it is not a minor
patch.

## Belt-editing discipline

- Edit the belt in place. Do not move a belt item into the core unless the
  commitment itself changes.
- When a belt adjustment and a core change both look available, prefer the
  belt adjustment; record the choice in `registry/AUXILIARIES.md`.
- Competing belt hypotheses (e.g., body-plan vs family-level kind width) are
  kept in competition, not resolved by assumption.
